Review and improve the existing “5-Minute Integrity Check” / check-in flow so it works better as a front-door tool for a man who has just been caught in problematic sexual behavior.

Goal:
The current 5-minute check-in needs to become more immediately useful for a man in acute crisis after discovery, disclosure, relapse, exposure, or confrontation.

Do not break or alter the existing daily check-in system for current IP users unless absolutely necessary. If the current 5-minute check-in is shared with daily check-ins, create a separate front-door version instead of changing the core daily check-in behavior.

Preferred new public-facing name:
“Caught Again? Start Here”

Subtitle:
“The First 24 Hours Integrity Check”

Purpose:
This tool is not a normal reflection exercise. It is an acute stabilization tool. It should help a man slow down, stop making things worse, tell the truth to himself first, avoid defensive or manipulative repair attempts, and take one clear next step toward accountability.

Key implementation instruction:
First, search the repo for the existing 5-minute check-in code, routes, components, prompts, database fields, AI prompt templates, completion flow, and any marketing/landing-page references. Identify whether this check-in is:

* a standalone public/free tool,
* part of AI Coach,
* part of daily check-ins,
* tied to user accounts,
* tied to onboarding,
* tied to lead capture,
* or reused across multiple places.

Make the smallest safe change that accomplishes this new front-door tool. Preserve current behavior everywhere else.

Public opening screen copy:
Title:
“Caught Again? Start Here”

Subtitle:
“The First 24 Hours Integrity Check”

Intro body:
“You may feel panic, shame, anger, fear, or the urge to explain. This is not the moment to perform, defend, bargain, minimize, or make big promises.

This check will help you slow down, tell the truth, and choose your next right step without making more damage.”

Safety notice:

Add a clear button:
“Begin the Check”

Clinical guardrails screen:
Before the questions, show:

“For the next 24 hours, do not:

* Pressure her to forgive you.
* Demand that she calm down.
* Ask her to comfort you.
* Blame stress, marriage problems, loneliness, or her reactions.
* Minimize what happened.
* Delete evidence.
* Add new partial truths after being confronted.
* Make promises you have not yet earned.
* Contact the other person.
* Try to force a relationship decision today.”

Then continue button:
“I understand. Continue.”

Question flow:
Use a short, structured, 5-step flow.

Step 1: What just happened?

Prompt:
“What were you caught doing, or what did you disclose?”

Options:

* Porn use
* Messaging/chatting
* Emotional/sexual contact
* Affair behavior
* Paid sexual content/services
* Hidden app/account/device behavior
* Relapse after a promise
* Lying/trickle truth
* Other

Free-text prompt:
“What is the most honest one-sentence description of what happened?”

Important:
Do not label this as “your side of the story.” Keep it factual.

Step 2: What are you tempted to do right now?

Prompt:
“What are you most tempted to do right now?”

Options:

* Defend myself
* Explain why it happened
* Minimize it
* Blame her reaction
* Promise it will never happen again
* Delete or hide more information
* Demand reassurance
* Shut down or disappear
* Contact the other person
* Hurt myself
* Use again
* Other

Safety behavior:
If the user selects “Hurt myself,” “Use again,” threats, danger, or indicates immediate safety risk, show a higher-risk support message and crisis resources. Do not continue with normal coaching as if nothing is wrong.

Suggested safety message:
“Your safety matters more than finishing this check. If you may harm yourself or someone else, or if anyone is in immediate danger, contact emergency services or a crisis line now. Do not stay alone with this.”

Use any existing crisis-resource component if one already exists in the app. Do not create conflicting crisis language if the app already has a standard crisis-resource pattern.

Step 3: What might she need from you right now?

Prompt:
“What might your spouse/partner need most from you in the next 24 hours?”

Options:

* Space
* Truth without pressure
* No more discoveries
* No defensiveness
* A written timeline later
* A commitment to get outside help
* Practical safety
* Transparency
* To not be responsible for my emotions
* Other

Important:
Do not ask, “What do you need her to understand?” This step should move him away from self-protection and toward empathy and responsibility.

Step 4: What is your next honest action?

Prompt:
“In the next 24 hours, I will:”

Options:

* Stop arguing and give her space
* Write down the factual truth before speaking further
* Contact my counselor/mentor/sponsor
* Begin the free AI Coach check-in
* Schedule an intake
* Remove access to the acting-out pathway
* Avoid contact with the other person
* Sleep somewhere else if needed for safety/stability
* Not ask for forgiveness today
* Not demand a decision from her today
* Other

Step 5: What can you say without making it worse?

Provide a suggested statement the user can copy, edit, or save:

“I know I have hurt you. I am not going to pressure you to calm down or forgive me. I am going to stop explaining and take responsibility for getting help. I will give you space, and I will not ask you to carry my emotions right now.”

Allow the user to edit this statement if appropriate.

Completion screen:
Title:
“You completed the First 24 Hours Integrity Check.”

Body:
“Your next step is not another promise. Your next step is structure.

For the next 24 hours, focus on truth, space, accountability, and not making the damage worse.”

Show the selected next honest action back to the user.

AI response behavior:
If this flow uses an AI-generated response, update the AI prompt so the response is:

* direct,
* stabilizing,
* clinically grounded,
* non-shaming,
* non-excusing,
* non-defensive,
* focused on the next 24 hours,
* not overly therapeutic,
* not overly religious,
* not overly long.

The AI should not:

* help the man craft excuses,
* help him manage his spouse’s emotions,
* tell him how to get forgiveness,
* encourage immediate disclosure dumping,
* encourage trickle truth,
* encourage him to demand reassurance,
* minimize betrayal impact,
* overpromise recovery outcomes.

The AI should:

* reflect the factual behavior,
* name the immediate risk pattern,
* reinforce responsibility,
* recommend one next honest action,
* remind him not to pressure his spouse,
* encourage outside support,
* point him to AI Coach / Integrity Protocol as the next structured step.
